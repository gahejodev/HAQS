Uppercase, lowercase, title case and sentence case explained

Consistent capitalization makes headings, labels and documents easier to read. The best style depends on the type of text and the style guide you follow.

Uppercase

UPPERCASE USES CAPITAL LETTERS FOR EVERY LETTER. It is common for short labels, abbreviations and some interface buttons. Long passages in all caps can be harder to scan, so reserve it for short text.

Lowercase

lowercase uses small letters throughout. It can suit informal styling, usernames or technical strings, but ordinary sentences still need standard capitalization for names and sentence beginnings.

Title case

Title Case Capitalizes The Main Words In A Heading. Rules vary: some styles keep short articles, conjunctions and prepositions lowercase unless they appear at the beginning or end. Check the publication's preferred style.

Sentence case

Sentence case capitalizes the first word and proper nouns, while other words remain lowercase unless grammar requires otherwise. It is often a clear, readable choice for headings and interface text.

Review names and acronyms

Automatic conversion cannot always identify product names, abbreviations, scientific terms or personal names. After converting, review items such as “NASA”, “iPhone” and names with unusual capitalization.

Frequently asked questions

Which case should I use for website headings?

Choose one style and use it consistently. Sentence case is often straightforward; title case can suit editorial headings when applied consistently.

Will a case converter fix grammar?

No. It changes capitalization only. It does not proofread, translate or rewrite your text.

Can title case rules differ?

Yes. Different editorial style guides capitalize short words differently. Follow the style required by your organization or publisher.

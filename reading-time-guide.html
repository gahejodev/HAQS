WRITING GUIDE

How to estimate reading time for an article

A reading-time label gives visitors a quick idea of the time they may need. It is an estimate, not a promise about how quickly every person reads.

The basic reading-time formula

A simple estimate divides the number of words by an assumed reading speed in words per minute (WPM). For example, a 900-word article at 225 WPM takes 4 minutes: 900 ÷ 225 = 4.

Choosing a reading speed

Reading speed changes with the reader, language, familiarity with the subject, layout and purpose. A person skimming a familiar topic may read faster than someone studying technical instructions. Use a reasonable default for a general audience, then adjust it when you know more about the intended readers.

Why estimates differ

  • Technical terms and unfamiliar vocabulary can slow readers down.
  • Tables, code, diagrams and images may require extra attention.
  • Short paragraphs and headings can make a page easier to scan.
  • Some readers skim while others read every sentence carefully.

Calculate your article's reading time

Paste your text into the HAQS reading time calculator. You can adjust the words-per-minute setting and see the estimate update. Use it as a planning aid, not a precise measurement.

Frequently asked questions

Is 225 words per minute correct for everyone?

No. It is simply the default assumption in this calculator. Actual reading speeds vary widely.

Should I include headings in the word count?

For a rough estimate, count the full text readers are expected to read, including headings. For complex layouts, add time for tables, diagrams or exercises as appropriate.

Does reading time affect search rankings?

A reading-time label is mainly a usability aid. Do not add one expecting it to guarantee a ranking improvement.

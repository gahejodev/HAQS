Keyword density explained: check phrase frequency without keyword stuffing

Keyword density describes how often a term appears in a piece of text compared with the total number of words. It can help you notice repetition, but it is not a score you need to maximise.

What does keyword density mean?

In a simple exact-match calculation, keyword density is the number of occurrences of a phrase multiplied by the number of words in that phrase, divided by the total word count, then multiplied by 100. For example, if a two-word phrase appears four times in a 500-word draft, its phrase-word share is (4 × 2 ÷ 500) × 100 = 1.6%.

How to check a keyword or phrase

  1. Choose the exact word or phrase you want to inspect.
  2. Paste your draft into the HAQS keyword density checker.
  3. Enter the phrase and run the check.
  4. Review the number of exact consecutive matches and the calculated share.
  5. Read the draft aloud and decide whether repeated wording sounds natural.

Why there is no magic percentage

A fixed density target cannot tell whether a page answers a searcher's question, uses the right terminology or provides a satisfying explanation. Search engines understand pages using many signals and language patterns. A percentage is only a narrow description of repeated text, not a reliable prediction of ranking.

What is keyword stuffing?

Keyword stuffing is the practice of loading a page with keywords or numbers in an attempt to manipulate search rankings. It can make sentences awkward and less useful. Avoid repeating a phrase where it does not belong, adding hidden keyword lists, or forcing the same wording into every heading.

A more useful editing checklist

  • Use the main phrase naturally in the page title and a clear main heading when it accurately describes the page.
  • Explain the topic with relevant terms a reader would expect.
  • Use synonyms only when they improve clarity; do not add them as a mechanical checklist.
  • Answer the reader's likely follow-up questions.
  • Remove repetition that adds no new information.

Frequently asked questions

What keyword density should I aim for?

There is no universal percentage that guarantees better rankings. Aim for natural, clear writing that fully addresses the topic.

Does this checker analyse related keywords?

No. HAQS checks exact consecutive phrase matches. It does not provide search volume, competitor analysis, semantic coverage or ranking predictions.

Can I use it for a long article?

You can paste your draft into the browser-based checker. For very large documents, your device and browser may affect responsiveness.

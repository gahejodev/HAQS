How to create clear, readable URL slugs

A URL slug is the human-readable part of a web address that identifies a page. A good slug helps visitors understand what a link is about before opening it.

What makes a useful slug?

  • Describe the page: choose words that accurately reflect its main topic.
  • Keep it concise: remove filler words when they do not add meaning.
  • Use hyphens: separate words with hyphens so the address is easy to scan.
  • Use lowercase: consistent casing avoids confusing variations on some servers.
  • Avoid unnecessary symbols: keep punctuation and tracking fragments out of the slug.

Example

A page titled “10 Tips for Better Sleep!” could use the slug better-sleep-tips. It is shorter and easier to read than a slug containing every word and punctuation mark.

Should you change an old URL?

Be careful. Existing links may be bookmarked, shared, or referenced by other websites. If you change a published URL, set up a permanent redirect from the old address to the new one wherever your hosting platform allows it. Update internal links and your sitemap too. If you cannot manage redirects, consider whether the change is worth the risk of broken links.

Frequently asked questions

Do slugs need to contain the exact target keyword?

Use a natural description of the page. Do not force awkward keyword repetitions into the address.

Can a slug contain numbers?

Yes, when the number is meaningful and likely to remain accurate. Avoid dates or counts that will quickly become outdated unless they are essential to the page.

Does a shorter slug always rank better?

No. Clarity and accuracy matter more than making every URL as short as possible.
